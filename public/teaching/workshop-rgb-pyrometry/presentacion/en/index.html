By Kirchhoff's law soot emits at each colour what it absorbs, so we start with absorption. It is made of spheres, the primaries, 15 to 40 nanometres across. x_p compares their size with the wave and lies between 0.07 and 0.19. In the Rayleigh regime each primary absorbs according to its volume. C abs, its absorption cross section, is the area of shadow it casts. E(m) comes from the refractive index m. Kappa, the absorption per metre, is primaries per cubic metre times that area. f_s is that number times the volume of one sphere. Our group uses an E(m) of 0.297, measured at 650 nanometres. Other authors use 0.4 plus or minus 0.1. Next, aggregates.
The primaries stick together in open clusters, the aggregates. The one on the left grows to 60 primaries, and N_p is that number. We use RDG-FA, the approximation of Rayleigh, Debye and Gans for fractal aggregates, branched clusters that look alike at every scale. Each primary absorbs as if it were alone, so the aggregate absorbs N_p times more, the line of slope one in the graph. Exact calculations give 10 to 30 percent more for large aggregates. Kappa is still primaries times shadow area, and writing it with f_s cancels the diameter. That gives the equation in the box. Scattering, light deflected without emission, grows with a slope up to two and matters little. With this kappa we write the emission.
With kappa, the absorption per metre, we write the emission. Each ring of radius r and width dr has one T and one f_s. If the soot is at the gas temperature, the local emission j_lambda is kappa times Planck at the ring temperature. Its unit includes the steradian, which measures solid angle. Hot gas emits in the infrared, so the visible light comes from the soot. The graph is the spectrum of the ring, and the dashed line is that curve divided by its value at 650 nanometres. More soot raises the curve and the dashed line stays. More temperature raises it and tilts it. So the shape gives T without knowing the soot. Now we follow it to the camera.

Next, what each level of calibration delivers.
The absolute calibration knows K_R and K_G and gives T and the soot in parts per million. Blue is not used, because K_B failed the test. The relative one knows only K_R over K_G, and it serves if the furnace emissivity is uncertain, because emissivity multiplies every channel alike and cancels. It gives T and a relative soot. Uncalibrated, with the nominal psi_j, it gives a T and no soot. That T can carry a large bias and is only a guide. Without a confirmed exposure time or millimetres per pixel, the soot is provisional.
